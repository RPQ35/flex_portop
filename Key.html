1. Core Concepts & Real-World Analogies

Understand database keys by mapping them to everyday objects and identification systems.

Standard Field

Normal Attribute

Any data field in a relation that does not uniquely identify a record on its own. Multiple records can share the exact same value.

Analogy:

Your Hair Color or First Name. Many people can have brown hair or be named "John".

Uniqueness Guaranteed (May have Redundancy)

Super Key

Any combination of one or more attributes that uniquely identifies a row in a table. A super key can contain extra, unnecessary attributes (redundancy).

Analogy:

{Passport Number, Full Name, Age}. It uniquely identifies you, but Name and Age are redundant extra baggage!

Minimal Super Key

Candidate Key

A super key with no redundant attributes. If you remove any single attribute from a candidate key, it loses its ability to uniquely identify rows.

Analogy:

Your Passport Number alone. Remove any digit and uniqueness is lost. It is pure and minimal.

2. Key Hierarchy & Inclusion Relationship

Every Candidate Key is a Super Key, but not every Super Key is a Candidate Key. Normal attributes live outside the key universe.

Candidate Keys are Minimal

They sit at the core. Each candidate key is the smallest possible unique identifier combination.

Super Keys wrap Candidate Keys

Any candidate key plus any normal attribute forms a super key. They guarantee uniqueness but include extra attributes.

Normal Attributes are Non-Unique

Attributes like Name or Age cannot identify rows by themselves and cannot form keys without unique identifiers.
